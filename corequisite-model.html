What is ACME?

ACME: Alignment and Completion of Math and English

As defined by the Board of Regents, ACME is a CT State Community College policy with the goal to maximize the probability that each student will enter and complete gateway, college-level, transferable coursework in English and mathematics within one year, or 24 credits, of initial enrollment.

Policy Goals

ACME aims to achieve its mission through:

  • Faculty-led curriculum design that places students directly into gateway, college-level, transferable courses with corequisite supports as needed rather than into prerequisite developmental sequences
  • Faculty-led placement processes to more accurately identify student corequisite support needs
  • Eliminating the completion gap in gateway coursework, specifically the gap that exists for Black and Latinx students when compared to White students

Equity Statement

This policy commits CT State Community College to ensuring that all policies, practices, and procedures related to placement and student success in gateway English and mathematics courses are designed to be anti-racist, eliminate structural inequities, recognize and address implicit bias, and promote equitable course completion.

The policy emphasizes that each student receives the specific support they need, rather than a one-size-fits-all approach, in order to maximize their success in college-level English and mathematics.

Key Premise

Research shows that traditional prerequisite courses hinder students' progress and raise, rather than lower, barriers to gateway, college-level, transferable course completion. The corequisite model replaces developmental prerequisites with embedded support, allowing students to enter directly into credit-bearing courses.

The ENG 1010/0910 Corequisite Course

The ENG 1010/0910 corequisite model pairs ENG 1010 with the linked ENG 0910 support workshop for students whose placement indicates that additional writing support will be beneficial. ENG 0910 provides just-in-time support connected to ENG 1010 reading and writing work.

ENG 1010: Composition

3 Credits

Satisfies: Written Communication I General Education requirement

Description: Composition focuses on the study and practice of effective written communication across a variety of rhetorical situations. The course develops skills in applying language conventions, engaging with and using authoritative sources, and crafting logical arguments.

All students enroll in ENG 1010 as the gateway composition course.

ENG 0910: Composition Workshop

0 Credits

Satisfies: Does not satisfy General Education credit

Description: This is a linked support section for students enrolled in ENG 1010. In this workshop, students receive additional instruction in support of English 1010 outcomes, specifically critical reading, thinking, and writing; crafting logical arguments; using language effectively; and engaging credible, college-level sources.

Students are placed into the linked workshop through the current CT State placement process.

ENG 0910 Course Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course and in alignment with English 1010 student learning outcomes, students will be able to:

  • Apply critical reading strategies for understanding, analyzing, and interpreting texts
  • Apply writing strategies for developing thesis-driven essays with textual support
  • Apply language and documentation conventions consistently
  • Apply affective skills that support the reading and writing processes
  • Develop independent reading, writing, and revising processes

Workshop Activities

Course outcomes might be realized through some or all of the following:

  • Critical reading, writing, and documentation activities in support of assignments in ENG 1010
  • Discussion in support of understanding and meeting the requirements of ENG 1010 assignments
  • Guided workshop and structured peer review in support of ENG 1010 assignments
  • Facilitation of metacognitive reflection on reading and writing processes
  • One-on-one conferences
  • Integrated affective lessons in areas such as growth mindset, self-efficacy, motivation, time management, collaboration, and goal setting
  • Introduction and appropriate referral to support services and campus resources

Attendance, Participation & Grading

Attendance

Important: Since CT State is not an attendance-taking institution, faculty cannot penalize students for non-attendance. However, if a student fails, faculty will need to document the student's last date of attendance when entering final grades, so keeping an attendance record is recommended.

Although points cannot be subtracted for non-attendance, instructors can incentivize participation through assessment activities that require active engagement during class time.

Grading Scenarios

At the end of the semester, there are four grading scenarios for the 1010/0910 corequisite:

1 Pass ENG 1010 + Participate in ENG 0910

Result: Students receive a letter grade in ENG 1010 and a P (Pass) in ENG 0910. This means students have earned a "C" or higher in ENG 1010.

2 Do Not Pass ENG 1010 + Participate in ENG 0910

Result: Students receive a grade of MP (Marginal Pass) in ENG 0910. This means students have earned a "C-" or lower in ENG 1010.

3 Pass ENG 1010 + Do Not Participate in ENG 0910

Result: Students receive an "F" in ENG 0910. Ideally these students have been dropped for NP (No Pass) or encouraged to withdraw.

4 Do Not Pass ENG 1010 + Do Not Participate in ENG 0910

Result: Students receive an "F" in ENG 0910. This means students have earned a "C-" or lower in ENG 1010 AND did not participate in the workshop.

Course Attempts

A course may be attempted twice but not a third time without advisement. The highest grade received will be used in calculating the student's academic average. This policy does not apply to courses designed to be repeated for additional credit.

Placement into ENG 1010/0910

Placement into ENG 1010 and ENG 0910 follows the current CT State placement process. GPA is used when available, with approved standardized measures used when appropriate. Accuplacer information is retained for students with historical scores.
