Welcome to ENG 1010

Welcome to ENG 1010: Composition at CT State - Three Rivers. Whether you are teaching the course for the first time or returning to it, this guide is intended to provide a practical starting point while leaving room for instructors to make thoughtful choices about readings, assignments, classroom activities, and course design.

ENG 1010 focuses on effective written communication across a variety of rhetorical situations. Students learn to respond to rhetorical situations, engage with and use authoritative sources, craft logical arguments, and apply language conventions. The resources in this guide are designed to help instructors meet those shared course outcomes while developing a course that reflects their own teaching approach and the needs of their students.

Quick Start Checklist

Before Your First Class

  1. Review the official course description and learning outcomes in the Course Overview.
  2. Familiarize yourself with the required text: They Say / I Say with Readings, 6th ed. (Norton, 2024).
  3. Review sample assignment prompts and rubrics in Assignments and Assessment.
  4. Use the Course Planning Guide as a sample framework for organizing the semester.
  5. Email your syllabus to the department chair and the dean's office within two weeks of the start of the semester.

Guiding Principles for Teaching ENG 1010

Writing as Process

We teach writing as a recursive process of drafting, revising, and editing—not as a product transmitted from instructor to student. Assignments should include opportunities for invention, peer review, and substantive revision.

Equity and Access

Students enter ENG 1010 with different educational experiences, responsibilities, and levels of preparation. Equity does not mean lowering the shared course outcomes; it means creating clear and accessible pathways for students to reach them. Thoughtful scaffolding, transparent expectations, accessible course materials, and opportunities for practice and revision can help students engage fully with college-level reading and writing.

Rhetorical Awareness

Students develop as writers by understanding how purpose, audience, and context shape communication. Assignments emphasize rhetorical situations rather than generic essay forms.

Critical Reading and Thinking

Reading and writing are intertwined. Students learn to read complex texts critically, evaluate arguments and evidence, respond to ideas, and synthesize perspectives across multiple sources. These practices help students develop their own positions and integrate source material purposefully into their writing.
