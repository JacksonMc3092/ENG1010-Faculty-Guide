Navigating CT State Policies

As a CT State faculty member, you're responsible for understanding and implementing various institutional policies. This page provides practical guidance on key policies that affect your daily work in ENG 1010, including FERPA (student privacy), class cancellations, grade reporting, and student success initiatives.

🔒 FERPA: Student Privacy & Educational Records

The Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA) is a federal law that protects the privacy of student education records. As a faculty member, you handle FERPA-protected information daily, so understanding your obligations is critical.

What is Protected Under FERPA?

Education Records Include:

  • Grades and academic performance
  • Course schedules and enrollment information
  • Student ID numbers
  • Disciplinary records
  • Financial information (billing, financial aid)
  • Class attendance records
  • Disability accommodations
  • Any information directly related to a student maintained by the institution

FERPA Dos and Don'ts for Faculty

✅ DO:

  • Use CT State email for all student communications
  • Post grades in Blackboard (secure, password-protected)
  • Discuss student performance privately (in person, via phone, or secure email)
  • Verify identity before releasing information
  • Keep graded papers and exams secure
  • Use student ID numbers (not names) on public postings if necessary
  • Refer students to the Registrar for official transcripts

❌ DON'T:

  • Post grades publicly with names or full ID numbers
  • Leave graded work in public places for student pickup
  • Discuss a student's performance with other students
  • Share student information with parents without written consent (even for students under 18 in college)
  • Use personal email for discussing grades or confidential information
  • Disclose that a student is enrolled in your class to third parties
  • Post class rosters with identifying information publicly
⚠️ Important: Once students turn 18 OR enroll in college (whichever comes first), FERPA rights transfer from parents to students. You cannot share educational information with parents without the student's written permission, even if parents are paying tuition.

Common FERPA Scenarios in ENG 1010

Scenario 1: Parent Calls About Student's Grade

What to say: "I understand your concern, but I'm not able to discuss your son/daughter's academic performance without their written permission due to federal privacy laws (FERPA). I'd be happy to speak with [student name] directly. They can complete a FERPA release form in the Registrar's Office if they'd like me to communicate with you about their grades."

Scenario 2: Returning Graded Papers

Best practice: Hand back papers individually during class, return them in sealed envelopes, or have students pick them up during office hours. Do not leave them in a stack outside your office where other students can see names and grades.

Scenario 3: Discussing Student with Disability Services

This is allowed: You may discuss accommodations with the Office for Disability and Accessibility Services (ODAS) when a student has requested accommodations. This falls under "legitimate educational interest." However, the student must initiate the accommodation process.

Scenario 4: Student Email from Personal Account

Best practice: Respond only to CT State email addresses. Politely redirect: "Please resend this from your CT State email address so I can respond. This helps me verify your identity and protect your privacy."

💡 Recording Classes & FERPA: If you record synchronous class sessions and post them in Blackboard, be aware that student voices/images are part of education records. Use the consent process outlined in the syllabus template and do not share recordings outside your Blackboard course.

❄️ Class Cancellations & Weather Policies

Official Cancellation Policy

Only the Chief Executive Officer (CEO) of the college can officially cancel or delay classes due to weather or emergencies. Faculty do not have authority to cancel college operations.

How Students Are Notified:
  • MyCTState Alerts - Text/email notifications (students should sign up: MyCTState Alert Signup)
  • CT State website - Homepage banner announcements
  • Local TV stations - News crawl during severe weather

What Faculty Should Do

If Classes Are Officially Cancelled:

  • Post an announcement in Blackboard acknowledging the cancellation
  • Provide make-up assignments or plan for make-up sessions
  • Adjust your syllabus schedule if needed
  • Do not penalize students for absence on cancelled days

If YOU Need to Cancel Class (Personal Emergency):

  1. Notify your Department Chair or Program Coordinator immediately
  2. Post announcement in Blackboard so students see it
  3. Email students (use Blackboard messaging or email roster)
  4. Plan make-up work - Online assignment, rescheduled class, or extended office hours
  5. Document the cancellation and make-up plan for your records
⚠️ Attendance Policy Reminder: Even when you cancel class, students are responsible for keeping up with course material. Provide asynchronous work or alternative assignments so students don't fall behind.

Make-Up Strategies for Cancelled Classes

  • Asynchronous Modules: Post a lecture video or reading with discussion questions in Blackboard
  • Additional Assignment: Extra low-stakes writing assignment or reading response
  • Extended Office Hours: Offer optional review sessions during usual office hours
  • Peer Review Online: If you miss a workshop day, move peer review to Blackboard discussions
  • Adjust Due Dates: Push back major deadlines by one class session if cancellation affects work time

📊 Grade Reporting & Academic Standing

Final Grade Submission

Faculty are required to submit final grades by the deadline specified in the Academic Calendar. Late grade submission delays students' registration for subsequent terms and affects their financial aid.

Grade Submission Process

  1. Log into Banner Self-Service via my.ctstate.edu
  2. Navigate to Faculty Services → Final Grades
  3. Select the appropriate term and CRN
  4. Enter final letter grades for each student
  5. Submit grades before the deadline

Important: Grades in Blackboard are NOT automatically transferred to Banner. You must manually enter final grades in the Banner system.

Grade Changes

If you need to change a grade after submission (due to calculation error, late work accepted, etc.), you must complete a Grade Change Form.

Grade Change Process:
  1. Obtain a Grade Change Form from your Department Chair or Registrar
  2. Complete the form with justification for the change
  3. Obtain Department Chair signature
  4. Submit to Registrar's Office
  5. Keep a copy for your records

Note: Grade changes require administrative approval and should be rare. Double-check all calculations before submitting final grades.

Incomplete Grades ("I")

See the Syllabus Templates page for the complete Incomplete grade policy. Key points:

  • Faculty are not required to grant Incompletes
  • Incompletes should be granted only for extenuating circumstances (illness, family emergency)
  • Student must have completed at least 60% of coursework
  • Student must complete work by 10th week of next standard semester
  • Use the official Incomplete Contract Form documenting requirements and deadline
⚠️ Warning: An "I" is NOT a substitute for a "W" (withdrawal). Students who simply stop attending should not receive an Incomplete—they should withdraw or receive a failing grade.

📅 Academic Engagement Policy

CT State has an Academic Engagement Policy that requires students to begin participation in each class by a specific date (usually end of the first week). Students who do NOT show evidence of engagement must be dropped from the course.

What Counts as "Academic Engagement"?

  • Attending class (in-person or synchronous online)
  • Submitting an assignment
  • Completing a quiz or assessment
  • Participating in a discussion board
  • Emailing the instructor with a course-related question
⚠️ Important: Simply logging into Blackboard is NOT sufficient evidence of engagement. Students must actively participate in course activities.

Faculty Responsibilities

What You Must Do:

  1. Monitor attendance/participation during the first week
  2. Report non-engaged students to the Registrar by the specified deadline
  3. Document engagement (attendance records, Blackboard activity logs)
  4. Include engagement policy in syllabus (required statement provided in syllabus template)
ℹ️ Why This Matters: The engagement policy protects students from being charged for classes they never attended. It also ensures compliance with federal financial aid regulations, which require students to "begin attendance" to receive aid.

⚖️ Student Code of Conduct

The CSCU Student Code of Conduct outlines expected behavior and the disciplinary process for violations. As a faculty member, you're responsible for maintaining an environment conducive to learning.

Common Conduct Issues in the Classroom

Disruptive Behavior

Examples: Excessive talking, using phone during class, arriving late repeatedly, verbally attacking other students or the instructor

What to do:

  1. Address it directly but privately (speak to student after class)
  2. Reference syllabus expectations
  3. Document the conversation
  4. If behavior continues, contact your Department Chair
  5. For severe disruption, file a Student Conduct Report (available through Student Affairs)

Academic Dishonesty

Already covered under Academic Integrity policy (see Syllabus Templates). Faculty can assign a failing grade for the assignment or course.

Threatening or Concerning Behavior

If a student exhibits threatening behavior or you're concerned about their safety/wellbeing:

  • Immediate threat: Call Campus Security or 911
  • Concerning behavior (not immediate threat): Contact the CT State Care Team or Dean of Students
  • Mental health concerns: Refer student to Wellness Services
💡 Best Practice: Establish clear behavioral expectations on Day 1. Include a statement in your syllabus about respectful classroom conduct. Prevention is easier than intervention.

🎯 Supporting Student Success

CT State offers extensive support services for students. As a faculty member, you play a key role in connecting students to these resources.

Key Support Services

📚 Tutoring & Academic Support

All campuses offer free tutoring in writing, reading, and study skills.

Refer students to: Campus Tutoring Centers (in-person and online options)

For ENG 0910 students: Embedded tutors are available during workshop sessions

🧠 Mental Health & Wellness

Wellness Counselors provide short-term counseling and referrals

Services: Stress management, anxiety/depression support, crisis intervention

Link: ctstate.edu/wellness

♿ Disability & Accessibility Services

Office for Disability and Accessibility Services (ODAS) provides accommodations for students with documented disabilities

Faculty role: Implement approved accommodations; do not request documentation from students directly

Link: ctstate.edu/ODAS

🗺️ Academic Advising

Guided Pathways Advisors help students with course selection, degree planning, and career exploration

When to refer: Students unsure of major, considering withdrawal, struggling with multiple courses

📚 Library Resources

CT State Library offers research assistance, databases, and study spaces

For ENG 1010: Librarians can provide research instruction sessions for Essay 3

Link: ctstate.edu/library

Early Alert & Student Outreach

Many campuses use an Early Alert system to identify and support struggling students. If your campus uses this system:

  • Submit alerts for students who are struggling academically or disengaging (typically by Week 4-6)
  • Advisors and support staff follow up with alerted students
  • Continue monitoring and provide updates as needed
ℹ️ Proactive Support Works: Students who receive early intervention are significantly more likely to persist and succeed. Don't wait until they're failing—reach out when you notice warning signs.
