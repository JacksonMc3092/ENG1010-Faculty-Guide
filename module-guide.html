About This Guide

Purpose and Flexibility

The ENG 1010 Course Planning Guide offers one possible framework for organizing the semester. The sections identify common goals, topics, activities, and points of emphasis that can help instructors address the course learning outcomes.

Important: This framework is a planning resource, not a required sequence. Instructors may adapt the timing, organization, readings, assignments, and activities to reflect their teaching approach and the needs of their students. Concepts may be introduced, revisited, or developed at different points in the semester. What matters is that students have meaningful opportunities to practice and demonstrate the ENG 1010 learning outcomes.

Best Practice: Demystifying the Process

Even though instructors provide students with a course calendar that outlines upcoming readings and assignments, students are often not aware of the "big picture."

  • Let students know how a reading, assignment, or learning task is relevant to the course goals
  • Be explicit about how previous work connects to new assignments
  • Highlight how current activities will help with upcoming work
  • Emphasize learning as a recursive process

Close Reading, Critical Analysis, and Understanding the Conversation

Suggested Weeks 1–5

Module Focus: During this module, particularly in weeks 1-2, many instructors focus on building rapport in the classroom using personal introductions and/or "ice breakers." Such activities can establish the tone for the semester and begin building a respectful and safe space for students to share and explore ideas.

Instructors introduce the course syllabus, course calendar, and quick starter lessons such as how to access online course resources, correctly format documents in MLA, and brief discussions of time management and course planning. Instructors may begin introducing or developing the first major writing project during this period.

Establishing Your Writer's Presence

Suggested Weeks 6–7

Students begin to develop a clearer presence in their writing by identifying their position, responding to other perspectives, and making deliberate choices about how they enter a conversation of ideas.

Instructors reinforce that academic writing usually addresses a problem or issue within a topic and that good academic writing seeks answers to meaningful questions.

Exploring the Conversation: Inquiry & Research

Suggested Weeks 8–11

Module Focus: This section of the course typically moves students toward becoming active researchers. Instructors introduce strategies for finding and integrating outside research material and students begin writing using outside sources.

Instructors usually schedule a library orientation session at this point in the semester. However, one library session will not be enough to help students navigate library resources. Instructors should plan on continuing to assist students in finding appropriate sources and strategies for reading scholarly source material.

Writing Towards Discovery: Research & the Power of Questions

Suggested Weeks 12–15

Module Focus: Generally, students are working on their extended research project at this stage. The importance of developing good questions for research is highlighted in this unit.

We ask students to engage in the complexity of topics they are researching—moving beyond dualistic constructions of "for/against" or "good/bad"—and learn that often academic scholarship leads to more questions rather than simplistic, concrete answers.

Important Reminders

Journal Responses

While informal and usually ungraded, journals should be more than simple "I agree/I disagree" or "I really liked..." personal responses even if using the first person. Encourage students to dig deeper to explore the ideas and concepts emphasized in the course material. Journals can be used as springboards for class discussions or primarily as a place for the instructor and student to begin a dialogue during conferencing.

For ENG 0910 Workshop Sections

The 0910 workshop time should be used to support activities from the 1010 course. Consider the following:

  • Use workshop time for targeted instruction on areas where students struggle
  • Provide additional time for drafting and revision
  • Schedule one-on-one conferences with students
  • Work with the embedded tutor to provide differentiated support
  • Integrate metacognitive reflection activities
  • Practice reading strategies with complex texts

Remember: The 0910 workshop should not include additional formal assignments beyond what is required in ENG 1010.
