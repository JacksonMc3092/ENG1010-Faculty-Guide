Assessment Approaches

Assessment in ENG 1010 should balance formative feedback that helps students develop as writers with summative evaluation of their work. The resources on this page offer sample rubrics, grading approaches, and feedback strategies that instructors may adapt to their own course design while remaining aligned with the ENG 1010 learning outcomes.

Key Principle: Students should have clear criteria for assessment and meaningful information about their strengths, areas for development, and opportunities for improvement.

Sample Essay 1 Rubric: Close Reading & Critical Analysis

Assignment: 3-5 pages analyzing a text's rhetorical strategies and argument. Assesses students' ability to move beyond summary into analytical argumentation.

Sample Essay 1 Rubric (100 points total)
Criterion Excellent (A) Proficient (B) Developing (C) Emerging (D/F)
Thesis & Argument
(25 points)
 23-25 pts: Thesis is clear, specific, and arguable. Makes an insightful claim about the text's rhetorical strategies or argument. Essay consistently develops and supports this claim. 20-22 pts: Thesis is clear and arguable but may lack specificity or nuance. Essay generally supports the claim with occasional lapses in focus. 17-19 pts: Thesis is present but may be too general, obvious, or not fully arguable. Essay partially develops the claim but includes irrelevant material. 0-16 pts: No clear thesis, or thesis is purely descriptive/summary. Essay lacks argumentative focus.
Evidence & Analysis
(30 points)
 27-30 pts: Uses specific, well-chosen textual evidence. Analysis explains HOW and WHY evidence supports the argument. Moves beyond summary to interpretation. 24-26 pts: Uses relevant textual evidence. Analysis is present but may occasionally be superficial or rely on summary. Generally explains significance of evidence. 21-23 pts: Uses some evidence but may be too general or under-analyzed. Relies heavily on summary. Analysis is present but underdeveloped. 0-20 pts: Little or no textual evidence. Relies entirely on summary. No meaningful analysis of how/why evidence matters.
Organization & Coherence
(20 points)
 18-20 pts: Essay has clear introduction, body, and conclusion. Paragraphs are focused and logically sequenced. Transitions guide readers smoothly through argument. 16-17 pts: Essay is generally well-organized with identifiable structure. Some paragraphs may lack focus or clear topic sentences. Transitions are present but sometimes abrupt. 14-15 pts: Organization is present but uneven. Paragraphs may lack clear focus or logical order. Weak or missing transitions make connections unclear. 0-13 pts: Essay lacks clear organization. Paragraphs are unfocused or randomly ordered. No effective transitions.
Style & Mechanics
(15 points)
 14-15 pts: Writing is clear, engaging, and appropriate for academic audience. Few or no errors in grammar, punctuation, or mechanics. Sentences are varied and effective. 12-13 pts: Writing is generally clear and appropriate. Some errors in grammar/mechanics but they don't impede understanding. Sentences are mostly effective. 10-11 pts: Writing is sometimes unclear or awkward. Multiple errors in grammar/mechanics that occasionally interfere with meaning. Sentence structure needs work. 0-9 pts: Writing is frequently unclear. Patterns of error in grammar/mechanics significantly impede understanding. Sentence structure is problematic.
MLA Format & Citation
(10 points)
 9-10 pts: Consistently correct MLA format (header, pagination, in-text citations, Works Cited). Quotations are smoothly integrated. 8 pts: Generally correct MLA format with minor errors. Most quotations are well integrated. 7 pts: Some MLA errors. Quotations may be awkwardly integrated or improperly cited. 0-6 pts: Major MLA errors or missing citations. Quotations are poorly integrated or missing citations.
ℹ️ Using This Rubric: Share this rubric with students when you assign Essay 1. Consider having them self-assess using the rubric before submitting. This promotes metacognition and helps students internalize criteria for success.

Sample Essay 2 Rubric: Synthesis & Argument

Assignment: 5-7 pages synthesizing multiple sources to develop an original argument. This sample assignment asks students to respond to different perspectives, synthesize sources, and develop their own position within a conversation of ideas.

Sample Essay 2 Rubric (100 points total)
Criterion Excellent (A) Proficient (B) Developing (C) Emerging (D/F)
Thesis & Argument
(25 points)
 23-25 pts: Thesis presents a clear, original argument that synthesizes multiple perspectives. Goes beyond agreeing/disagreeing to forwarding, complicating, or applying ideas. Argument is sophisticated and insightful. 20-22 pts: Thesis presents a clear argument that engages with sources. May be less sophisticated or original but still makes a meaningful contribution to the conversation. 17-19 pts: Thesis is present but may simply agree/disagree with sources without synthesis. Argument lacks originality or depth. 0-16 pts: No clear argumentative thesis. Essay summarizes sources without taking a position.
Synthesis of Sources
(30 points)
 27-30 pts: Skillfully synthesizes 3+ sources to identify patterns, tensions, or gaps. Avoids serial summary. Sources are in conversation with each other and support the writer's original argument. 24-26 pts: Synthesizes 3+ sources competently. Generally avoids serial summary. Most sources support the argument, though connections could be stronger. 21-23 pts: Attempts synthesis but may fall into serial summary ("X says this, Y says that"). Sources are present but not fully integrated into the writer's argument. 0-20 pts: Little or no synthesis. Essay primarily summarizes sources separately. Fewer than 3 sources used effectively.
Evidence & Analysis
(20 points)
 18-20 pts: Uses specific, well-chosen evidence from sources. Analysis explains significance and connects evidence to the writer's argument. Quotations are smoothly integrated and properly contextualized. 16-17 pts: Uses relevant evidence from sources. Analysis is present but may be less developed. Most quotations are well integrated. 14-15 pts: Evidence is present but may be too general or under-analyzed. Some quotations are awkwardly integrated or lack context. 0-13 pts: Little evidence or poorly chosen evidence. Analysis is minimal or absent. Quotations are improperly integrated.
Organization & Coherence
(15 points)
 14-15 pts: Essay is logically organized around the argument, not the sources. Paragraphs are focused with clear topic sentences. Smooth transitions create coherence. 12-13 pts: Essay is generally well-organized. Most paragraphs are focused. Transitions are present but could be smoother. 10-11 pts: Organization is uneven. May be organized by source rather than by argument. Some paragraphs lack focus. Weak transitions. 0-9 pts: Essay lacks clear organization. Paragraphs are unfocused. No effective transitions.
Style & Mechanics
(10 points)
 9-10 pts: Clear, engaging academic writing. Few or no errors. Varied sentence structure. Appropriate tone and voice. 8 pts: Generally clear writing. Some errors but they don't impede understanding. Appropriate academic tone. 7 pts: Writing is sometimes unclear. Multiple errors that occasionally interfere with meaning. 0-6 pts: Writing is frequently unclear. Patterns of error significantly impede understanding.
💡 Synthesis vs. Summary: The key difference between Essay 1 and Essay 2 is synthesis. Help students understand that synthesis means putting sources in conversation with each other, identifying patterns and tensions, not just summarizing each source separately.

Sample Essay 3 Rubric: Research-Based Argument

Assignment: 6-8 pages making an original, research-based argument using 6-8 credible sources. This sample project provides students with an opportunity to integrate and demonstrate multiple ENG 1010 learning outcomes through sustained research-based writing.

Sample Essay 3 Rubric (100 points total)
Criterion Excellent (A) Proficient (B) Developing (C) Emerging (D/F)
Research Question & Thesis
(20 points)
 18-20 pts: Research question is focused, complex, and genuinely inquiry-driven. Thesis presents an original, sophisticated argument informed by research. Demonstrates critical engagement with scholarly conversation. 16-17 pts: Research question is clear and appropriate. Thesis presents a solid argument supported by research. Engages meaningfully with sources. 14-15 pts: Research question may be too broad/narrow or not fully developed. Thesis is present but may lack sophistication or originality. 0-13 pts: No clear research question. Thesis is absent, purely descriptive, or not research-informed.
Source Quality & Integration
(25 points)
 23-25 pts: Uses 6-8+ high-quality, credible sources (including 3+ scholarly). Sources are diverse in type and perspective. Quotations and paraphrases are smoothly integrated and properly contextualized. 20-22 pts: Uses required number of credible sources. Mix of scholarly and popular sources. Most are well integrated, though some may be less smoothly incorporated. 17-19 pts: Uses minimum sources but quality may be uneven. May lack scholarly sources or source diversity. Integration is awkward at times. 0-16 pts: Insufficient number of sources or uses unreliable sources. Poor integration. May rely heavily on one or two sources.
Synthesis & Analysis
(25 points)
 23-25 pts: Masterfully synthesizes research to support original argument. Analysis is sophisticated, connecting research to broader implications. Acknowledges complexity and multiple perspectives. 20-22 pts: Competently synthesizes research. Analysis connects sources to argument. Acknowledges different perspectives. 17-19 pts: Attempts synthesis but may fall into serial summary. Analysis is present but underdeveloped. Limited engagement with complexity. 0-16 pts: Little synthesis. Essay primarily summarizes research. Minimal analysis or critical engagement.
Organization & Development
(15 points)
 14-15 pts: Essay is expertly organized with clear introduction, body, and conclusion. Each section serves the argument. Paragraphs are well-developed with strong topic sentences and transitions. 12-13 pts: Essay is well-organized overall. Most paragraphs are focused and developed. Transitions are generally effective. 10-11 pts: Organization is uneven. Some paragraphs lack focus or development. Transitions need work. 0-9 pts: Essay lacks clear organization. Paragraphs are poorly developed. Weak or missing transitions.
Style, Mechanics & MLA
(15 points)
 14-15 pts: Clear, engaging, sophisticated academic writing. Few errors. Perfect or near-perfect MLA format (in-text citations and Works Cited). Signal phrases introduce sources effectively. 12-13 pts: Generally clear academic writing. Some errors but they don't impede understanding. MLA format is generally correct with minor errors. 10-11 pts: Writing is sometimes unclear. Multiple errors. Some MLA errors in citations or Works Cited. 0-9 pts: Writing is frequently unclear. Patterns of error impede understanding. Major MLA errors or missing citations.
ℹ️ Scaffolded Assessment: For Essay 3, consider grading intermediate steps (proposal, annotated bibliography, rough draft) for completion or low-stakes points. This encourages students to follow the research process while reserving high-stakes grading for the final product.

Grading Strategies & Principles

Balancing Rigor with Equity

Effective grading maintains high standards while recognizing that students enter ENG 1010 with vastly different preparation and experiences. These strategies promote both excellence and equity.

Weighted Grading

Sample Course Grade Breakdown:

  • Essay 1: 15-20%
  • Essay 2: 20-25%
  • Essay 3: 25-30%
  • Low-stakes writing (discussions, quick writes, drafts): 15-20%
  • Course engagement and process work: 10-15%

Rationale: Emphasizes major essays while giving credit for process and engagement.

Revision Options

Consider allowing:

  • One major revision per semester
  • Revisions only for essays earning C+ or lower
  • Grade averaging (average original + revision)
  • Required revision memo explaining changes

Benefit: Emphasizes growth and learning over single performances.

Specifications Grading (Optional Approach)

Pass/No Pass with clear specifications:

  • Define what "passing" work looks like for each assignment
  • Students can revise until work meets specifications
  • Reduces subjective grading
  • Makes expectations transparent

Grade Distribution & Standards

Grade Percentage What This Grade Means A (93-100) Excellent Work exceeds expectations in all or nearly all areas. Demonstrates sophisticated understanding and application of course concepts. Writing is clear, well-organized, and compelling. A- (90-92) Excellent Work is strong in all areas with minor areas for improvement. Demonstrates clear mastery of course learning outcomes. B+ (87-89) Very Good Work is consistently strong and meets expectations. Demonstrates solid understanding and competent application of concepts. B (83-86) Good Work is generally strong with some areas for improvement. Demonstrates good understanding of most course concepts. B- (80-82) Good Work meets expectations in most areas. Some inconsistency in quality or understanding of concepts. C+ (77-79) Satisfactory Work meets basic expectations but lacks depth or consistency. Demonstrates adequate understanding of core concepts. C (73-76) Satisfactory Work meets minimum expectations. Demonstrates basic understanding but significant room for growth. C- (70-72) Satisfactory Work barely meets minimum expectations. Major areas need improvement. D+ (67-69) Passing Work falls below expectations in multiple areas. Demonstrates limited understanding of course concepts. D (63-66) Passing Work is weak in most areas. Minimal understanding of course concepts. D- (60-62) Passing Work is consistently weak. Little evidence of understanding course concepts. F (0-59) Failing Work does not meet minimum expectations. Does not demonstrate understanding of course concepts OR work was not submitted.
💡 Setting Expectations: Share your grade distribution and rubrics on Day 1. When students understand what "A work" vs "C work" looks like, they're more likely to aim for excellence. Consider showing model essays (with permission) to illustrate different grade levels.

Efficient Feedback Workflows

Grading 20+ essays takes time. These workflows help you provide meaningful feedback without burning out.

The "Comment Bank" System

Keep a document of frequently used comments you can copy/paste or customize. Organize by category:

Sample Comment Bank Categories

  • Thesis Issues: "Your thesis needs to be more specific and arguable. Try using a 'because' clause to explain why your claim matters."
  • Evidence Problems: "You're summarizing here instead of analyzing. Push deeper: WHY is this evidence significant? What does it reveal?"
  • Organization: "This paragraph introduces a new idea that deserves its own paragraph. Consider breaking this into two focused paragraphs."
  • Citation Errors: "Remember to introduce quotations with signal phrases that provide context for your readers."
  • Mechanical Issues: "You have a pattern of comma splices. Review the handout on comma usage and consider visiting the Writing Center."

Grading Workflow (For One Set of Essays)

Step 1: Quick Sort (30 min)

Skim all essays and sort into rough categories:

  • Strong (A/B range)
  • Middle (C range)
  • Weak (D/F range)

This gives you a sense of the overall class performance.

Step 2: Grade Strong Essays First

Start with strong essays to calibrate what excellence looks like. These are also faster to grade because there are fewer errors to mark.

Step 3: Use Rubrics Consistently

Circle or highlight scores on the rubric. Add brief marginal comments on 2-3 key paragraphs. Write a 3-5 sentence end comment summarizing strengths and priorities for improvement.

Step 4: Take Breaks

Grade in batches of 5-7 essays with breaks in between. This maintains consistency and prevents "grading drift" where your standards change as you tire.

💡 Time-Saving Tip: For low-stakes assignments (discussion posts, quick writes, rough drafts), consider grading for completion with brief comments rather than detailed feedback. Save your energy for major essays where detailed feedback has the most impact.

Portfolio Assessment: An Optional Approach

Some instructors use a portfolio approach where students compile and reflect on their work at the end of the semester. This emphasizes growth over time and gives students agency in representing their learning.

Portfolio Structure

What to Include in a Composition Portfolio

  • Final drafts of all three major essays (revised as needed)
  • Selected low-stakes writing (3-5 pieces)
  • Evidence of revision (marked-up drafts, before/after comparisons)
  • Reflective cover letter (3-4 pages) analyzing growth as a writer
  • Optional: Annotated table of contents explaining choices

Reflective Cover Letter Prompts

The cover letter is where students demonstrate metacognition about their growth. Provide guiding questions:

  • What are your strengths as a writer? How have they developed this semester?
  • What aspects of writing are still challenging for you?
  • Choose one essay you're particularly proud of. What makes it successful?
  • Choose one essay you struggled with. What did you learn from that struggle?
  • How has your understanding of [course theme] evolved?
  • How have you grown in your ability to read, analyze, and use sources?
  • What writing skills from this class will you use in future courses or careers?
ℹ️ Portfolio Grading: You might weight the portfolio as 30-40% of the final grade, replacing individual essay grades or supplementing them. Grade holistically using a portfolio rubric that assesses the collection as a whole plus the quality of reflection.

Assessing Students Enrolled in ENG 0910

Students enrolled in ENG 0910 receive a separate grade for the workshop according to the ENG 0910 grading guidelines outlined in the Corequisite Model section. Their ENG 1010 work is assessed according to the same ENG 1010 learning outcomes and academic standards as other students. The additional workshop time provides support to help students meet those expectations.

Key Principles for Corequisite Assessment

Same Standards, More Support

ENG 0910 students are held to the same learning outcomes as all ENG 1010 students. The workshop provides additional support to help them meet those standards.

Emphasize Growth

ENG 0910 students may start the semester further from learning targets. Acknowledge and celebrate growth even if the final product isn't yet at the A/B level.

Use Formative Assessment

The workshop allows for extensive low-stakes practice. Use frequent check-ins, quick writes, and draft feedback to monitor progress.

Coordinate with Embedded Tutor

The embedded tutor can help with assessment by providing additional feedback on drafts, facilitating peer review, and identifying students who need extra support.

ℹ️ Grading Scenarios for ENG 0910: Remember that students' final grades in both ENG 1010 and ENG 0910 affect their progression. See the Corequisite Model page for details on grading scenarios and what they mean for student progression.
